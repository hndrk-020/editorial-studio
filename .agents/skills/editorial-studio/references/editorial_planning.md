# Editorial planning and editing pass

Use this after the existing brief and creative-direction approval. Reuse information already supplied; ask only about missing details that materially affect the publication. Planning and editing are passes within the existing roles, not additional departments, agents or approval gates.

## Plan the publication

Create a compact working plan before drafting features:

- **Editorial promise:** one sentence stating what this issue helps its intended reader understand, experience or decide.
- **Issue theme:** the connecting idea and boundaries that keep the stories focused.
- **Story mix:** each feature's distinct angle and contribution; identify overlap and combine or differentiate repetitive stories.
- **Flatplan:** page or section order, story purpose, estimated copy length, image needs and transitions. Distinguish actual PDF pages from feature slots; respect the renderer's existing magazine and onepager limits.

Sequence stories for a clear opening, development and ending. Vary depth and image emphasis where the supported format permits. For a onepager, use a short content outline and its existing feature capacity instead of a magazine-scale plan. Treat page allocations and word budgets as estimates until layout confirms fit.

Use this reusable outline, omitting fields that do not help the project:

```text
Editorial promise:
Issue theme and boundaries:
Audience and desired outcome: [reuse brief]
Approved direction and brand-sheet reference:

Page/section | Story angle and reader takeaway | Approx. words | Image need | Source status

Opening and closing logic:
Unresolved editorial questions:
```

Keep this working document alongside the project, for example `editorial_plan.md`. Record unresolved claims without inventing evidence. Update the plan when research or layout changes the story; do not add manifest fields or claim the dashboard consumes this document.

## Edit before quality review

After research-backed drafting and image selection, review the copy in this order:

1. **Story and structure:** check that each piece delivers its planned takeaway, opens with a clear angle and develops logically. Remove repetition across the issue and unsupported promotional language.
2. **Voice and line editing:** apply the publication's brand sheet, improve clarity and rhythm, and retain the subject's specific details. Preserve the meaning of sourced statements and the strength of their evidence.
3. **Editorial furniture:** make headlines accurate and distinct; use introductory text, subheads and pull quotes only where the layout supports them. Pull quotes must come from the actual text or a verified quotation. Captions should add verified context rather than describe only what is visible; retain credits.
4. **Copyediting and evidence:** check names, dates, numbers, spelling, punctuation, terminology and consistency. Recheck claims affected by rewriting against their sources. Never invent interviews, quotations, reporting or citations; retain material uncertainty.
5. **Layout readback:** inspect the exported pages for reading order, headline breaks, caption association, overflow and copy fit. Shorten or restructure text without discarding essential qualifications or shrinking type merely to force a fit. Repeat the existing export and QA checks after layout-affecting corrections.

Finish with a brief working record of substantive edits and unresolved editorial questions. This supports, and does not replace, the existing fact verification, asset approval, preflight, PDF audit and visual inspection. Do not describe unresolved checks as completed or treat polished prose as verification.
