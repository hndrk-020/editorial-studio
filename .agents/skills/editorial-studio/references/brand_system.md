# Reusable publication brand sheet

Use a compact brand sheet to carry a publication's identity across issues. Read existing brand guidance and previous approved issues first. Reuse established decisions and permissions; do not infer rights to fonts, logos or photographs from their presence in a reference.

For a new publication, draft the sheet from the brief and finalize its visual decisions through the existing two-direction selection. For an established publication, develop both required directions within its enduring identity, varying issue treatment rather than silently redesigning the brand. If the brief calls for a brand change, explain the specific tradeoff through the existing creative-direction gate. This sheet creates no additional approval round.

## Reusable sheet

```text
Publication name:
Brand-sheet version/date and basis: [provided guide, approved direction or previous issue]
Audience and editorial promise:
Personality: [a few specific traits]
Voice: [tone, terminology and short examples of preferred phrasing]

Enduring identity
- Masthead/logo: approved asset, placement and clear-space guidance where supplied
- Typography: title/body/caption roles, available fonts and practical fallbacks
- Palette: HEX values and each color's role
- Photography: recurring subject treatment, mood and crop principles
- Recurring sections and navigation: where relevant and supported
- House style: language variant, capitalization, dates, numbers and credits

Issue-specific treatment
- Theme and approved direction:
- Allowed variation: accent color, cover treatment, image mood or other flexible elements
- Project-specific exceptions and rationale:

Asset locations and usage conditions:
Open decisions or implementation limits:
```

Separate supplied requirements and approved choices from provisional suggestions. Keep the sheet alongside the publication project, for example `brand_sheet.md`; reuse and update it for later issues without overwriting the established identity inadvertently. Keep prior decisions traceable when the identity changes.

## Apply the sheet

- Use it during concept development, writing, image selection and the editing pass. A consistent identity should still allow appropriate changes in pacing and composition.
- Distinguish desired design specifications from what the current renderer can implement. Flag unavailable fonts, unsupported masthead positioning or custom layouts rather than claiming the brand sheet enables them.
- During existing visual QA, compare the cover and story pages against the sheet for typography, color roles, voice and caption treatment. Resolve unintended drift while preserving approved issue variations.
- Existing image authenticity, rights, sourcing and release rules continue to apply independently of brand consistency.
